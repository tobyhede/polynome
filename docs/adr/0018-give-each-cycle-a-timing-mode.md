# Give each Cycle a Timing mode

Every Cycle carries a Timing mode, either Polymeter or Polyrhythm, deciding how its Rhythm layers share time. Polymeter is the existing behaviour and the default for a new Cycle: every layer's Signature unit lasts `60 / BPM`, and the Cycle span is the least common multiple of the layers' Meter numerators. Polyrhythm makes the Cycle span one Meter of the Cycle's first Rhythm layer, and every other layer fits its own numerator of Signature units into that same span.

The two modes read the same Configuration. A Timing mode edit changes no Meter, no Subdivision, and no Step voice; it changes only what the numbers already there are worth in seconds. A Cycle holding `4/4` and `3/4` is three bars against four on one shared pulse in Polymeter, and four against three in one shared bar in Polyrhythm, and switching between them loses nothing and is reversible.

Polynome's first version could sound three against two by giving two layers the same Meter and different pattern lengths, because a step lasted the Meter's duration divided by the raw pattern length. The meter-relative grid replaced that arithmetic and the ability went with it. This returns the ability as a declared relationship between layers rather than one that emerged from a pattern length nobody named.

## Consequences

- Timing mode belongs to the Cycle, beside its repetitions and its BPM envelope, and is part of the Configuration: it saves into a Preset, marks a setup unsaved, and survives a reload. A Sequence may step from a Polymeter Cycle into a Polyrhythm one. No Cycle holds both relationships at once, because Polyrhythm makes every Meter in the Cycle one length while Polymeter makes every Signature unit one length, and three simultaneous layers cannot have it both ways.
- BPM keeps its definition exactly: Primary beats per minute. In Polyrhythm it is the first Rhythm layer's beat, and no other layer's Signature unit is a Primary beat. This is a narrowing of [ADR-0002](0002-sequence-complete-cycle-spans.md)'s global tempo, not a departure from it: there is still one tempo curve from one Transport origin, and still no independent clock.
- A Cycle span is derived in Polymeter and declared in Polyrhythm. Both satisfy the span's definition — the shortest duration after which every layer returns to its downbeat together — but Polyrhythm satisfies it by construction rather than by arithmetic over the Meters.
- The meter-relative grid is unchanged in both modes: `numerator × subdivision` Pattern positions. A Timing mode edit therefore writes no Canonical pattern, unlike the Meter-numerator, Subdivision and Display mode edits of [ADR-0012](0012-reset-the-pattern-on-a-meter-or-subdivision-edit.md). It has the `restart-transport-run` consequence, like every other timing-structure edit.
- `Beat N` still labels the Nth Signature unit of a layer's Meter in both modes, because the count is what a listener counts across the bar. Only the unit's written name stopped being true.
- Removing a Cycle's first Rhythm layer promotes the next one and re-clocks every layer that remains. No rule guards it and no warning precedes it. The reference is the first layer because there is no reordering to make that fragile, and naming it as a settable property would promise a choice that does not exist.
- Switching a Cycle to Polyrhythm shortens it: `4/4` and `3/4` go from a twelve-beat span to a four-beat one. Repetitions are not adjusted to compensate, and a BPM envelope over that Cycle spends itself over the shorter span.
- Pulse density is unbounded beyond the existing Meter, Subdivision and tempo ranges. A one-beat first layer under a `16/4` layer at Subdivision five and 300 BPM reaches four hundred pulses a second. That is a reachable, audible, self-inflicted result of two controls, and it is recoverable by changing either; a rejection rule would make a Meter numerator's validity depend on another layer's.
- In Polyrhythm, every Rhythm layer after the first loses its Meter denominator control, because the first layer's Meter and this layer's numerator already fix its unit and there is nothing left to choose. Those layers are labelled `3:4` — their numerator against the first layer's — wherever a Meter is written: the Rhythm heading, its settings, the Preset notation and summary, and the Meter-commit announcement. Their Subdivision names drop the written unit with it.
- The denominator those layers stop showing is still stored, still saved into a Preset, and returns unchanged when the Cycle returns to Polymeter.
- The interface labels the control `Poly` with segments `meter` and `rhythm`, so the label completes each value into its own word; the segments carry `Polymeter` and `Polyrhythm` as their accessible names. It sits above the BPM Envelope and shows on every Cycle, including one holding a single Rhythm layer, where it is latent rather than inert: it takes effect when a second layer arrives. This is the same deliberate split as the Beat control, where the interface says one thing and the vocabulary says another for a stated reason.
- A stored Cycle with no Timing mode repairs to Polymeter, which is what it already sounded like. The Configuration key does not change. This is repair replacing a missing value with a default, which [`AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md) names as the alternative to key retirement, and it is not a migration, a schema version, or a compatibility shim.
- The seeded examples become four: the existing `4/4 8ths` and `4/4 Triplets`, plus one Cycle of `4/4` and `3` seeded twice, once in each Timing mode. The difference between two identical Configurations under one switch is the only demonstration that explains the feature, and it is the payoff of [ADR-0010](0010-seed-example-presets-into-storage.md) putting examples into storage as ordinary Presets rather than into prose. The Preset key moves from `polynome-presets-v3` to `polynome-presets-v4` and v3 is retired, because Seeding only runs against a key that has never been written and a new example would otherwise reach nobody.
- [ADR-0015](0015-refine-defaults-and-sliders.md)'s consequence that the examples demonstrate two pulse densities of one rhythm rather than polymeter no longer holds. Its defaults, slider steps and Balance midpoint are untouched.
- [Meter validity and tempo reference](../research/meter-validity-and-tempo-reference.md) rejected non-dyadic denominators partly because, once the denominator stopped scaling the beat, `4/3` and `4/4` were one rhythm under two labels with nothing left to express. Polyrhythm gives them something to express: against a `4/4` first layer, a three-beat layer sounds three units of four-thirds of a quarter, which is `3/3` in exact-rational notation. The rejection stands on its remaining grounds — Polynome is a metronome and not a notation engine, the reachable denominators are a lumpy third of the numerator pairs in range, and the unit is derived from the relationship rather than chosen — but that one argument is retired.
